AI & innovation in EMS

Artificial intelligence in prehospital care isn't science fiction — it's in this year's journals

AI in EMS gets talked about in extremes: either it's going to replace clinical judgment, or it's overhyped vaporware. The research says something more useful — a set of specific, measurable tools that are already improving specific decisions. Here's what's actually published, not speculated.

Triage & transport decisions

Getting the right patient to the right hospital, faster

Deciding whether a patient needs a trauma center, a stroke center, or the nearest emergency department is one of the highest-stakes calls a crew makes — and traditional scoring tools miss more than clinicians would like. A 2025 narrative review of machine learning models for prehospital triage and destination decisions found that, across trauma, stroke, sepsis, and cardiac presentations, machine learning models consistently outperformed guideline-based scoring tools.1

Trauma-specific models cut undertriage below 10%, and stroke-detection algorithms reported accuracy scores above 0.98 — high enough to meaningfully change which hospital an ambulance heads toward. 1

Global & low-resource systems

AI is doing the most where EMS infrastructure is thinnest

A 2025 scoping review of AI use in low- and middle-income countries found that the most common application wasn't diagnosis at all — it was predicting call volume and dispatch demand, followed by classifying incoming calls and predicting disease risk. 2

In systems without the staffing to triage every call the way a well-funded urban service can, a model that forecasts where the next surge in calls is coming from can be the difference between having an ambulance ready and not.

Community-facing tools

AI support networks aimed at the public, not just the crew

Not every application lives inside the ambulance. Research published in the Journal of Medical Internet Research describes an AI-enhanced digital network designed to support bystanders and callers during the critical minutes before EMS arrives — a reminder that "prehospital care" now starts before a 911 call is even answered. 3

Where this goes next

An international consensus on AI's future in EMS

A 2026 Delphi consensus study gathered 27 international EMS experts over three rounds to forecast AI's near-term impact on the field. The panel reached agreement on 81 specific items spanning six domains: communication, clinical care, education, management, operations, and ethics.4

Notably, seven items didn't reach consensus — a useful reminder that even the experts don't agree on everything AI should touch in EMS, particularly around ethics and autonomy. 4

Building it ourselves

What this looks like at the grassroots level

Most AI-in-EMS research is happening at the health-system level — hospital networks, dispatch centers, national panels. This nonprofit's own tools are a smaller-scale, provider-built version of the same idea: retrieval-based reference assistants and adaptive study tools, built for individual students and crews rather than entire systems.
