ECG AI

A research prototype that turns an uploaded 12-lead ECG image into machine-readable waveforms and runs an experimental AI model over them — built to demonstrate the pipeline from image to waveform to model output, not to diagnose.

Launch ECG AI → Read the full technical report →
Educational / research prototype — not for clinical decision-making. This is an unvalidated experimental prototype. Waveform reconstruction from a photographed strip is inherently lossy, and model output must never be used for patient care decisions.
How to use it

Manual

1

Upload a 12-lead ECG image

Provide a photo or scan of a printed 12-lead strip. The system automatically detects and crops each of the 12 lead regions (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1–V6) from the full image.

2

Review the waveform reconstruction

The prototype traces each detected lead region into a machine-readable waveform. Because this step depends on image quality, angle, and print contrast, reconstruction quality varies — review each lead before trusting the output.

3

Read the experimental AI output

Once enough leads reconstruct successfully, the waveforms are passed to an experimental model. The output is clearly labeled as experimental and is not a substitute for a 12-lead read by a trained clinician.

4

Check the clinical reference panel

A differential-considerations panel surfaces possible considerations tied to specific observed findings — framed explicitly as reference material, not a diagnosis, and meant to be correlated with history, physical exam, and other data.
