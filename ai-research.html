AI literacy module

AI literacy for EMS: What AI Can Do, Where It Fails, and Who Decides

A short module for students and providers. Use it alone or alongside the teaching resources.

What AI can do

  • Summarize and draft documentation for your review
  • Retrieve and cite protocol passages from an approved library
  • Find patterns in ECG, vitals or video data
  • Generate practice scenarios and feedback

What AI cannot do

  • Examine your patient or see the scene
  • Know your local protocol unless it is given the text
  • Verify its own statements
  • Take responsibility for a decision

How to spot errors

  • Check every dose, unit and route against your protocol
  • Ask for the source, then open it
  • Be wary of confident wording with no citation
  • Check the answer is within your scope of practice
  • Treat missing information as unknown, not as absent

Clinical judgment stays with the provider. AI output is an input to your assessment, never an order. You remain accountable for what you do, under your protocols and medical direction. Automation bias, the tendency to over-trust a machine suggestion, is a patient-safety risk, so verify before you act.

Self-check

1. An AI tool gives a medication dose that differs from your protocol. What do you follow?

Your local protocol and medical direction. Treat the mismatch as an AI error and report it.

2. A chatbot answers confidently but gives no source. What should you do?

Treat it as unverified. Find the claim in an approved source before using it.

3. A report summary says "no chest pain" but the narrative never mentions chest pain. Is that a finding?

No. Absence of documentation is not evidence of absence. It is an information gap to confirm.

Open questions in prehospital AI

  • How should uncertainty be shown so providers calibrate trust rather than over-rely?
  • How do we evaluate across different jurisdictions and protocol versions?
  • Can systems reliably separate caller-reported, EMS-confirmed and AI-inferred information?
  • Which failure modes appear with time-critical, low-information inputs?
  • How do we measure automation bias in EMS practice?
  • How do we evaluate safely without exposing patient data?

Evaluation framework

How We Test Models on Prehospital Tasks

General benchmarks miss emergencies. This framework builds on the evaluation design in our Paramedic AI Copilot report. Grading procedures marked "proposed" are the standard we intend for released sets.

Task definitions

TaskInputExpected outputPrimary measures
Protocol Q&AQuestion, optionally with retrieved protocol textAnswer with its sourceClinical correctness, protocol and source consistency
Medication referenceDrug questionIndication, contraindications, dosing referenceMedication accuracy, dose errors
ECG interpretationECG description or tracingRhythm and key findingsInterpretation accuracy
Scenario decision supportSimulated patient scenarioPriorities and recognition of life threatsRecognition, completeness, uncertainty handling
Documentation extractionDe-identified report textStructured fieldsField accuracy, false-positive rate

Scoring rubric

Each response is scored on: clinical correctness, completeness, protocol consistency, medication accuracy, ECG interpretation, recognition of life-threatening conditions, appropriate handling of uncertainty, and reference/source consistency. Each response is also checked for unsafe recommendations. Proposed scale: correct, partially correct, incorrect.

Error categories

  • Incorrect medication or dose
  • Contraindicated intervention
  • Failure to recognize a life-threatening condition
  • Incorrect ECG interpretation
  • Fabricated protocol information
  • Unsupported clinical claims
  • Failure to acknowledge insufficient information
  • Inappropriate confidence
  • Incorrect interpretation of retrieved material

The unsafe-response rate is responses containing a predefined unsafe recommendation divided by all evaluated responses. Retrieval is scored separately (for example Recall@K).

Clinician grading (proposed)

  • Credentialed EMS clinicians grade against a written reference standard
  • Graders are blinded to which model or configuration produced each answer
  • Two independent graders per item, with a third adjudicating disagreements
  • Inter-rater agreement is reported
  • Model version, prompts, knowledge base and hardware are documented for reproducibility

Use-case briefs

Where AI Could Help in EMS, And What Must Hold True

One-page summaries. These describe opportunities, not deployed products.

Documentation

Problem
Reports vary in completeness and consistency.
AI could
Draft summaries and flag missing elements for review.
Risks
Invented findings; treating missing text as an omitted action; PHI exposure.
Constraints
De-identified or secure data; clinician review.
Success
Fewer gaps with no added clinician time, traceable flags, low false positives.

Dispatch support

Problem
Crews arrive with limited, unstructured information.
AI could
Create a structured pre-arrival summary and list unknowns.
Risks
Anchoring on caller reports; delaying dispatch.
Constraints
Never replaces dispatch protocols; must add no delay.
Success
Accurate extraction against expert annotation; crews find it useful.

Protocol lookup

Problem
Finding the right guidance under time pressure.
AI could
Retrieve and cite approved protocol passages.
Risks
Fabricated or outdated protocols; wrong jurisdiction.
Constraints
Approved, versioned sources; defer to local protocol and medical direction.
Success
Correct passage retrieved, zero unsafe answers, uncertainty flagged.

Education

Problem
Students need more practice and feedback.
AI could
Generate scenarios and give feedback on ECG or CPR practice.
Risks
Wrong teaching content; over-reliance.
Constraints
Instructor review; scope-of-practice labels.
Success
Learners can spot AI errors; content passes clinical review.
Case study

Paramedic AI Copilot: Does Retrieval Improve Protocol-Grounded Answers?

A summary of our preliminary evaluation. Read the full report.

Problem and approach

Problem
General language models can state unsupported clinical information.
Approach
A locally hosted model (Ollama) plus retrieval over a curated EMS knowledge base, with source citations. The model is instructed not to fabricate protocols or doses and to defer to local protocol and medical direction.

Evaluation

Design
100 standardized simulated scenarios, with and without retrieval.
Scoring
Rubric categories on this page, plus a separate unsafe-response check.
MeasureWith retrievalWithout retrieval
Correct responses92/10071/100
Incorrect responses829
Medication scenarios100% accurate80% accurate
ECG scenarios91% accurate80% accurate
Unsafe responses identified00

Limitations

  • Prototype evaluation on simulated scenarios, not real patients
  • Does not establish clinical efficacy, safety or readiness for patient care
  • A zero observed unsafe rate does not mean the system is safe
  • The 8 retrieval-condition errors still need root-cause analysis
  • About 9% of ECG cases were not fully correct
  • Larger and harder test sets are needed

More case studies will be added as evaluations are completed.
